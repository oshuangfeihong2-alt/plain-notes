Official open data sources

A short, hand-checked list. Every entry below is published by the organisation that owns the data, and each is released under public-domain, CC0 or similar terms. No scraped mirrors, no resellers.

Companies and finance

GLEIF — Legal Entity Identifier data
Every LEI record in the world, published as bulk files and a free API. Released under CC0. (gleif.org)
SEC EDGAR
Every filing by a US-listed company — annual reports, insider transactions, prospectuses. US government works, no copyright. Full-text search plus bulk archives. (sec.gov/edgar)
World Bank Open Data
Country-level indicators going back decades. Open under CC BY 4.0, attribution required. (data.worldbank.org)
UN Comtrade
Bilateral trade flows by commodity code and country. Free tier covers aggregated queries. (comtrade.un.org)

Intellectual property

USPTO Open Data Portal
Patent and trademark bibliographic data, assignments and full-text. US government works. (developer.uspto.gov)

Health and safety

openFDA
Drug adverse events, recalls, device reports and labelling. Public domain, with a documented API. (open.fda.gov)

Places and things

GeoNames
Every named place on Earth with coordinates and population, in one downloadable dump. Released under CC BY 4.0 — attribution is required, so read the licence before reuse. (geonames.org)
Wikidata
Structured statements about people, places and things, queried with SPARQL. Released under CC0 — no attribution needed. (wikidata.org)

A note on licence traps

Two things catch people out. First, attribution: CC BY and ODbL require you to credit the source, and ODbL is infectious — mix it into your own database and the whole database inherits the licence. Second, provenance: a dataset mirrored on a third-party site is not the same as the source dataset, and the mirror may have added terms of its own. When in doubt, go to the publisher.
